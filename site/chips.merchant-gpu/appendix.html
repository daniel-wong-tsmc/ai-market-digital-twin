How each dimension was rated

bottleneck — Weak

Memory now sets the ceiling: NVIDIA cut mainstream Rubin Ultra memory to 192GB from 288GB because stacked-memory supply is short, and silicon wafer lines run at full utilization. Packaging eased to a roughly 10% shortfall from 20%, leaving memory as the tighter of the two.

competitiveStructure — Mixed

AMD moved Helios racks into production and Google ordered more than three million Intel packaging units for its next accelerator, giving buyers real second sources. NVIDIA still takes the largest single commitments, including SpaceX building exclusively on its parts.

moat — Weak

Share estimates for NVIDIA still spread from 70% to 90%, and the loudest challenger claim, MediaTek's 15% to 20% target, drew analyst pushback from a base under 1%. Hyperscaler custom silicon already consumes roughly two-thirds of AI compute, holding the position where it was.

momentum — Very strong

Buyer commitments accelerated again: AMD data-center revenue doubled year over year to $6.7 billion, SpaceX raised nameplate compute to 1.4 gigawatts on $15.8 billion of quarterly AI capital spending, and Foxconn posted its first month above 900 billion Taiwan dollars. Alphabet's higher 2026 budget and the Korean memory group agreement carry that pull into 2027.

strategicRisk — Mixed

Both vendors lean on a handful of buyers: AMD names OpenAI and Meta at up to six gigawatts each, SpaceX expects a very significant share of NVIDIA's 2027 output, and three NVIDIA customers each account for 16% to 21% of revenue. US export limits still keep China revenue out of the plan.

unitEconomics — Strong

Vendors held premium margins through the ramp: NVIDIA at 75% non-GAAP, TSMC at 67.7%, Intel at 40.4% GAAP, and AMD at 54% GAAP against 40% a year earlier. AMD says accelerator margin runs below its corporate average, which caps further gains.
