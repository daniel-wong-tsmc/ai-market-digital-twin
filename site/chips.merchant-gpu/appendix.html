How each dimension was rated

bottleneck — Weak

NVIDIA can supply only about 70% of demand and Micron can fill roughly half of what customers ask, naming server DRAM the single largest constraint its hyperscale buyers face. The squeeze is physical: NVIDIA's forward supply commitments jumped to $279 billion mainly for memory, and it cut the flagship Rubin Ultra to 192 gigabytes from 288.

competitiveStructure — Mixed

AMD's Helios racks reached production and its flagship data-center accelerator now serves paying customers in Saudi Arabia, while OpenAI's first in-house inference chip and Marvell's raised custom-silicon outlook add buyers who design around merchant parts. Intel's memory-heavy inference GPU and MediaTek building on NVIDIA's own rack wiring widen the field of credible suppliers again this cycle.

moat — Weak

Custom chips already take about two-thirds of AI compute and grow 44.6% this year against 16.1% for merchant GPUs, so the 70-80% revenue share keeps eroding. Marvell winning custom work at Google and SK Hynix holding 58% of stacked memory show pricing power sliding toward suppliers and design partners.

momentum — Very strong

NVIDIA data-center revenue hit $89.0 billion, up 117% year over year, with the next quarter guided to $108 billion, while Broadcom AI silicon revenue rose 221% to $16.7 billion and AMD data-center revenue reached $6.7 billion, up 107%. Order evidence sits underneath the print: record Hewlett Packard Enterprise AI bookings of $3.1 billion, Nscale contracted revenue near $103 billion, and Korea's $919 billion state data-center programme.

strategicRisk — Mixed

Three direct customers make up 21%, 17% and 16% of NVIDIA revenue, and the largest backed block of demand rests on OpenAI, which lost $20.9 billion in 2025; NVIDIA has already cut its Ohio guarantee from $250 billion to under $120 billion. Outside financiers pledging over $500 billion soften that exposure, and the harder edge is policy: Taiwan indicted nine people over diverted AI servers and Washington is drafting tariffs on finished data-center hardware.

unitEconomics — Strong

Vendor margins still carry the category: NVIDIA ran at 75% earlier in the year, TSMC at 67.7%, and AMD lifted its reported margin to 54%. The slide has started, and memory is the cause: NVIDIA now guides 74% with a stated floor of 71-72% and management points to the low seventies by year-end.
