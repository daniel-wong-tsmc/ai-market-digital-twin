How each dimension was rated

bottleneck — Weak

Advanced packaging is loosening, with the capacity gap narrowing from about 20% to 10% by year-end as output ramps toward 125,000-130,000 wafers a month, while memory tightens: server contract prices are set to rise more than 25% in the third quarter and supply is booked out past 2027. Memory lead times of 32 to 40-plus weeks and power transformer waits averaging 128 weeks now set the pace of shipments.

competitiveStructure — Mixed

AMD put its 72-GPU Helios rack into production with shipments starting this quarter and Intel's foundry revenue grew 31% as it adds packaging capacity, giving buyers a second and third source. Custom accelerators are growing 44.6% against 16.1% for merchant GPUs, while AMD's own volume ramp slips to the second quarter of 2027, so the near-term field stays concentrated.

moat — Weak

NVIDIA's accelerator share is projected to slide from a 2024 peak of 87% to about 75% this year, and custom silicon at the largest buyers already takes roughly two-thirds of AI compute. Anthropic's early talks with Samsung about a 2nm custom chip show even anchor customers designing around the merchant part.

momentum — Very strong

The four largest US cloud buyers plan about $725 billion of 2026 capital spending, up 77% from last year, and Google Cloud's booked backlog rose more than $50 billion in one quarter to $514 billion. NVIDIA's record $75.2 billion data-center quarter and $91 billion guide for the next one show those orders converting into sales.

strategicRisk — Mixed

Three NVIDIA customers account for 21%, 17% and 16% of revenue, China data-center revenue fell to $4.55 billion from $9.66 billion, and NVIDIA more than halved its list of authorised Asian buyers. A growing share of demand now rests on NVIDIA guaranteeing or financing customer purchases, including a discussed $350 billion facility for one buyer, which ties sales to credit the seller extends.

unitEconomics — Strong

NVIDIA held a 75.0% gross margin and guided to the same level, TSMC posted 67.7%, and Intel's margin climbed to 40.4% from 27.5%. Sellers are passing rising memory and packaging costs through to buyers instead of absorbing them.
