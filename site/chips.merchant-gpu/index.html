This cycle adds H200-class licensing shifted from denial to case-by-case and buyer interest from Alibaba, ByteDance and DeepSeek, broadening the earlier MI325-only picture, while China revenue sits at $4,550M and H200 licenses have booked no revenue yet.
  • Prepaid HBM take-or-pay locks in accelerator demand - Multi-year high-bandwidth memory contracts that customers can't cancel — backed by billions of dollars in deposits — lock in a large share of future demand for AI chips, which lowers the odds that today's buildout is just speculation.
  • AMD growth leans on a few mega-deals - AMD's data-center accelerator growth depends on a small set of very large commitments, which concentrates both its upside and its downside in a handful of partners.
  • Vendor-financed demand circularity - More and more of the demand for Nvidia's AI chips is being funded by Nvidia's own revenue-sharing and financing deals. That means the demand strength Nvidia reports is increasingly tied to credit Nvidia itself is extending, adding "circular financing" risk to how durable this buildout really is.
  • Custom ASIC substitution - Custom in-house AI chips built by the largest cloud companies meaningfully cut into the growth of open-market GPU demand.
  • Pricing power persistence - Prices for renting GPUs, including pay-as-you-go rates, are staying strong even as more GPU supply comes online.
  • Backlog duration lengthens as demand is booked years ahead - Cloud and chip suppliers are signing longer-dated purchase commitments that push out the average life of their order books, so a growing share of AI compute demand is booked years in advance.
  • Rising memory costs inflate AI-server economics - Fast-rising memory and fast storage drives prices are pushing up what it costs to build each AI server, which could strain buyer budgets and squeeze system margins.
  • CUDA software lock-in sustains the moat - Because it's hard to run AI software written for Nvidia's chip software on non-Nvidia chips, customers stick with Nvidia even when rivals offer cheaper hardware.
  • AI packaging supply is concentrated in a few buyers - A few AI chip buyers control most of TSMC's advanced-packaging output, so any one of them cutting orders would leave capacity that is hard to refill.
  • Broadening AI demand tightens TSMC capacity and pricing - AI demand spreading across GPUs, custom ASICs, networking and power chips fills TSMC's capacity across nodes and lets it raise prices.

  • Multi-vendor accelerator supply wave in second-half 2026 - A simultaneous mass-production ramp of NVIDIA, AMD, AWS, and Google accelerators is set to expand total accelerator supply in the second half of 2026.
  • Neocloud GPU Buyers Grow Revenue but Deepen Losses - Fast-growing GPU rental cloud GPU buyers such as CoreWeave are booking record revenue while their net losses widen. (sources in history)
  • Cheaper inference pulls in demand - Each new generation of Nvidia chips lowers the cost of running AI models per unit of output, which pulls more AI workloads onto Nvidia's GPUs and widens the market Nvidia can serve.
  • NVIDIA holds a premium gross margin as it ramps new chips - NVIDIA keeps its gross margin near three-quarters even while ramping new products, far above rivals, showing buyers still pay premium prices.
  • Networking attach as systems moat - Nvidia now sells more data-center networking switches — the hardware that connects servers together — than any other company, and bundles that networking with its GPUs. This deepens its full-package advantage and makes it harder for customers to switch to standalone AI chips or custom-built silicon. In the first quarter of 2026, Nvidia took the top revenue spot in data-center networking switches by pairing its Spectrum-X and Quantum networking products with its Blackwell and GB300 chips, and its NVLink connection technology lowers the cost of running AI models per unit of output. However, no evidence found this run actually compares how fast networking sales are growing versus how fast chip sales are growing, so the stronger claim that networking is growing faster than computing is not backed up.
  • Hyperscaler AI spending may outrun near-term returns - Hyperscalers' sharp jump in AI capital spending risks a pullback if returns disappoint, which would cut accelerator orders.
  • Memory-based inference chips route around the packaging bottleneck - Intel's Crescent Island inference GPU uses standard LPDDR5X memory instead of high-bandwidth memory to sidestep the advanced-packaging and memory bottleneck, opening an alternative supply route for inference chips.
  • Model-provider revenue scales to underpin the buildout - Leading AI model providers are growing revenue fast enough that their heavy compute spending rests on real and rising sales rather than hope.
