MERCHANT GPU — Executive Brief

Tracks the merchant AI-GPU market — demand, supply, pricing, competition — and what it means one layer down: wafers, packaging, memory.

Monthly read: July 2026 (revision 12) · Last signal check: 2026-07-20

Strong / improving — Demand and vendor margins are very strong, so the single limit on shipments is physical: advanced packaging and memory allocation gates how much merchant-GPU reaches customers through 2027, and that gap is now narrowing.

Demand for AI accelerators runs at record levels, with vendor margins near 75 percent and hyperscaler spending set to climb about 75 percent this year, so the only thing holding shipments back is how fast advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory can be built. The rating turns on two questions: whether new packaging and memory lines close the supply gap on schedule through 2027, and whether the wave of custom in-house chips erodes merchant-GPU pricing faster than most buyers now assume. Watch packaging and memory lead times first, because any slip there caps how much silicon reaches customers before the demand doubts spreading through July markets harden into cancelled orders.

What this means for TSMC

  • Merchant-GPU demand runs very hot, with a record NVDA data-center quarter, hyperscaler capex climbing about 77%, and a 3.6M-unit B200 backlog signaling booked leading-edge wafer demand years out. Watch whether that demand holds if capex jitters spread. momentum evidence
  • Advanced packaging is the binding limit: CoWoS is sold out through 2027 on 52-78 week leads and HBM is sold out for 2026, so packaging, not wafers, caps realizable GPU revenue. Watch the ramp from about 75K toward 125-130K wafers a month. bottleneck evidence
  • The packaging gap is set to narrow from about 20% to 10% by end-2026 as two packaging suppliers add CoWoS capacity, pushing total supply toward roughly 200K wafers a month with outside packaging partners. Watch whether added capacity loosens allocation or just meets rising demand. bottleneck evidence
  • Custom ASICs are growing faster than merchant GPUs (44.6% vs 16.1%) and already run about two-thirds of AI compute, while Broadcom and Marvell control roughly 95% of that co-design. TSMC's leading-node base tilts toward hyperscaler custom silicon routed through two design houses. competitiveStructure moat evidence
  • The top three CoWoS clients take over 85% of capacity, with NVDA near 60% and AMD near 11% that AMD splits between its server processors and its accelerators. TSMC's advanced-packaging revenue leans on one dominant buyer. strategicRisk competitiveStructure evidence
  • Pricing stays firm across the chain, with TSMC's Q2 gross margin at 67.7% up 9.1 points, NVDA holding 70-80% share and pricing power, and HBM4 prices set to roughly double into 2027. Watch how much of that pricing power TSMC keeps versus what flows to memory suppliers. unitEconomics moat evidence
  • Competitive and policy moves are stirring: Intel is expanding Intel-3 in Ireland and pitching a Crescent Island part that skips CoWoS and HBM, while NVDA's China revenue halved to $4.55B under export limits.
