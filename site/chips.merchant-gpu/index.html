Strong / improving — Demand is accelerating and vendor margins hold near their highs, so the constraint that decides how much ships is physical supply: advanced packaging booked into 2027 and memory that has now tightened sharply.

Demand for AI accelerators is running at record levels and vendor margins are holding near their highs, so the only thing capping how much product ships is physical supply of advanced packaging and high-bandwidth memory. The next rating change turns on two questions: whether packaging capacity keeps closing its gap fast enough as memory becomes the tighter constraint, and whether the growing share taken by custom in-house chips finally dents the leading vendor's pricing power; our read stays more constructive than recent market talk of overspending, because booked orders and multi-year commitments still point up. The clearest early warning would show first in memory prices and lead times, where another sharp jump would signal that easing packaging supply is simply handing the constraint to memory rather than releasing it.

What this means for TSMC

  • Merchant-GPU demand momentum runs very strong, with hyperscaler capex guided up about 77 percent for 2026 and NVIDIA data-center revenue setting records, all of which pulls leading-node wafer demand higher.   • Advanced packaging stays sold out through 2027 with lead times of 52 to 78 weeks, yet the supply-demand gap is narrowing as CoWoS capacity climbs from about 75,000 toward 125,000 wafers a month.
