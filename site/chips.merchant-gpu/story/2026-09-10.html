The memory squeeze reaches China's own chips

Chinese chipmakers are raising prices because they cannot get enough stacked memory, the same shortage that keeps Nvidia from filling a third of the orders it sees.

1

China's alternatives get more expensive too

Chinese chipmakers, including Huawei and Cambricon,ⓘ raised their processor prices today because they cannot get enough high-bandwidth memory. Huawei's Ascend 950DT card has passed 250,000 yuan, a jump of 20 to 50 percent from quotes given about two months ago. Cambricon, MetaX and Iluvatar CoreX moved in the same direction.

The cause named in the reporting is the U.S. export controls put in place in December 2024, which cut Chinese buyers off from the main memory suppliers and pushed them into grey-market channels at several times international prices.

This matters because the home-grown Chinese parts were meant to be the way around a shortage of Nvidia chips. Instead they are stuck behind the same wall. The scarce thing is not the processor design; it is the memory that has to sit beside it.

The order book keeps growing anyway

OpenAI now plans to spend roughlyⓘ $750 billion on compute through 2030, described as a 25 percent increase on its own earlier estimates this year. That is one customer, raising its plan again, in a year when it already could not get what it wanted.

Nvidia's chief executive said this week that the company can supply only about 70 percent of the demand it sees, and OpenAI still calls itself really short on capacity. Nvidia also said it is working with Australian partners on a build-out of up to 2 gigawatts by 2027 to house its machines.

So the gap is not closing from the demand side. Every extra dollar of planned spending lands on the same queue.

A first hint that buyers have a limit

Kioxia, which makes the flash memoryⓘ that goes into these machines, raised prices 70 percent in the June quarter, on top of a prior quarter with increases above 100 percent. Its chief executive then said another 70 percent jump seems unlikely, because even the biggest cloud buyers have limited budgets.

That is the first plain statement from a supplier that there is a ceiling on how much cost can be passed along. It is a comment, not a price cut, and prices have not come down.

Meanwhile the factories keep loading. TSMC reported August revenue of NT$514.81 billion today, up 53.3 percent on the same month last year and 10.1 percent on July. Output is still climbing; what it costs to fill each machine with memory is climbing faster.

4

What to watch next

Watch whether the Kioxia comment becomesⓘ a pattern. If other suppliers also say buyers have hit their budget limit, the run of component price rises stops being one-way, and the cost of building a machine settles. Until then, a 70 percent quarterly rise is the going rate.

Watch the Chinese prices. Huawei's card above 250,000 yuan, up 20 to 50 percent in two months, is the clearest read on how scarce memory is outside the contracted supply. If those quotes keep climbing, nothing has loosened anywhere.

And watch for memory that does not come from the same three factories. Kepler Compute is marketing a memory technology aimed at inference, backed by $468 million raised, with samples this year and production in 2027. A working alternative would be the first real relief; a slipped date would mean another year of the same queue.
