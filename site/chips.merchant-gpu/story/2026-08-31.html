NVIDIA puts $279 billion on the table to buy memory

The company more than doubled what it has promised to pay suppliers, mostly for memory, and still tells investors it can only plan on half the growth its customers are asking for.

1

A very large cheque, written mostly for memory

NVIDIA's long-term supply commitments have risenⓘ to $279 billion over the next few years, up from $119 billion three months earlier. Of that, $92 billion falls due in the rest of fiscal 2027, with $87 billion and $88 billion in the two years after.

The jump is mainly memory: high-bandwidth memory and server memory for the Vera Rubin ramp. The same account says memory supply still cannot cover full customer demand. That is the plainest reading of why supply is not catching up. The money is committed; the parts are not there yet.

What the shortage costs, in growth and in margin

NVIDIA guided fiscal 2028 to 70%ⓘ revenue growth even though its finance chief said customer forecasts implied roughly 140%. The stated reason is that memory supply could not support the full demand behind those forecasts. On its August 26 call the company also said it has supply for about 70% of the demand it sees.

The shortage shows up in profit as well. Management has told investors that gross margin dips into the low 70% range by the end of fiscal 2027, because memory costs more.

Buyers keep paying someone else to design chips

Marvell, which designs custom chips forⓘ the big cloud companies, reported data centre revenue of $2.171 billion for its quarter, up 46% from a year earlier and 79% of its $2.739 billion total. It guided the current quarter to $3.150 billion, give or take 5%.

The forward numbers moved more than the reported ones. Wire coverage of the same results says Marvell raised its fiscal 2028 revenue outlook to about $18 billion from about $16.5 billion, with data centre revenue expected to grow more than 60% and the custom business to more than double. Earlier this month Google was reported to be adding Marvell-designed inference chips alongside its own in-house program.

4

What to watch next

The nearest date is mid-September, whenⓘ mediation is expected to begin between Micron and its Taiwan union. A preliminary tally at the Taoyuan and Taichung sites on August 29 found about 80% of employees backing strike action over the bonus system. No strike has been called and mediation is still pending, but Taiwan holds roughly 60% of Micron's worldwide production capacity, so the result matters beyond one company.

Then watch whether the money NVIDIA has committed turns into parts: $92 billion of those supply commitments falls due in the rest of fiscal 2027. Two policy threads sit underneath. Washington is reported to be preparing tariffs on imported chips that would reach finished data centre servers, with duty-free import allowances sized to a company's US plant investment; no rate and no start date have been set, and the account says the plan could change substantially. Separately, an unproven investigation into a freight forwarder over 47 NVIDIA server shipments to China has no primary government notice confirming it.
