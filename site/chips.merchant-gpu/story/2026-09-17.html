Rents go up again, and new racks squeeze more out of each megawatt

A cloud landlord raises chip rental prices for the second time since May, while the first measured results from Nvidia's newest racks show far more work per unit of power.

1

Renting a chip just got dearer again

Nebius will raise its pay-as-you-go rentalⓘ prices by roughly 17 to 21 percent on October 1. Its top current card goes from $7.85 to $9.50 an hour, a 21 percent step and as much as 56 percent more than it cost in May. Prices do not move like that when supply is catching up with demand.

The order book explains why. Nebius says customer demand is now visible more than 24 months out, with some reservations running into the first half of 2028, and its cloud arm booked $575 million of revenue last quarter, up 514 percent on a year earlier. Older hardware is where the cheap hours still are: the company's public price list shows an earlier mainstream card at $3.85 an hour, while a rival quotes a reserved 16-chip cluster at $9.86 per chip-hour.

2

The same power, more work

CoreWeave says it has brought upⓘ a multi-rack cluster of Nvidia's new Vera Rubin machines, linking hundreds of the chips together, and calls itself the first cloud provider to prove the design at rack scale. Its first measured result is the number that matters: 10 times more tokens per megawatt than the Blackwell generation it replaces. A token is the unit of work these machines produce when answering a question.

An independent measurement is more conservative and points the same way. SemiAnalysis, in a note dated September 14, clocks Rubin at 59.4 million tokens a second per megawatt against 28.5 million for the best prior configuration, and calls that about 7 times better per megawatt overall. Electricity is one of the hard limits on how much capacity gets built, so more work per megawatt is real added supply rather than a marketing claim.

The money arrives before the machines

Ten banks led by Goldman Sachsⓘ are lending $22 billion to Crux AI, the venture backed by Blackstone and Alphabet, with the loan secured on the chips themselves and on customer contracts. Crux will spend the money on Google's own accelerators and plans to bring its first 500 megawatts online in 2027.

Lenders willing to take chips as security tell you how confident they are that the machines will be busy. They also tell you the shortage is not a funding problem. In the same week, Nvidia, Google and Emerald AI launched an alliance on data-center power, aimed at standards for grid-friendly sites and quicker grid connections. No capacity or timing figures came with it.

4

What to watch next

First, whether October's higher rents stick.ⓘ Nebius's increase of roughly 17 to 21 percent takes effect on the first of the month. If buyers pay it without argument, scarcity is still setting the price.

Second, the rivals. Huawei has pulled its next accelerator forward by three quarters, to the first quarter of 2027, yet its rotating chairman says the company cannot satisfy even Chinese demand and has no plan for a full international rollout. Arm holds a public revenue target of $1 billion for its new data-center processor while saying customer demand is twice that. The alternatives are arriving short, not spare.

Third, memory. SK Hynix is in preliminary talks to make high-bandwidth memory at Intel's Ohio plant, which would be its first memory manufacturing in the United States. No investment size and no timeline were given, so treat it as a statement of intent rather than supply.
