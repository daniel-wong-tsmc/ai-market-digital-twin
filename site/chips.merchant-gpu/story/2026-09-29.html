A $518 billion promise, and prices still climbing

Memory is still what caps how many chips get built. Today's news was about who pays for them, and whether that money holds.

1

The price tags move again

A single Nvidia H100 card satⓘ in stock on Newegg today at a headline price of $34,989.00, with options from $29,500.00. The newer H200 board on the same page was shown as out of stock and coming soon, with no price at all. When the older part is the one you can actually buy, nothing has loosened.

Renting is no cheaper. RunPod's secure cloud now starts at $2.89 an hour for an H100 and runs to $6.79 for a B200, after a September repricing that lifted the middle and top of its list. Nebius has told customers its on-demand rates rise on October 1: the H100 from $3.85 to $4.50 an hour, the H200 from $4.50 to $5.40, the B200 from $7.15 to $8.50 and the B300 from $7.85 to $9.50. That is its second increase this year, which points to rentable capacity staying scarce rather than slack building up.

2

Memory is still the ceiling

Nothing today moved the physical limit.ⓘ Speaking on 9 September, Nvidia's chief executive said the company can currently supply only 70% of the demand it sees, with its largest customer still calling itself short of capacity.

The reason sits upstream of the chip. Reporting on 7 September put memory stockpiles at Samsung and SK hynix below ten days of supply, and forecasters expect memory demand next year to outgrow memory supply by more than ten percentage points. Memory is also taking over the budget: its share of AI infrastructure spending is seen reaching 57% by 2027, up from 14% in 2025, and high-bandwidth memory needs roughly three times the factory capacity of ordinary memory. Until that changes, more orders simply mean longer queues.

3

The biggest buyer's bill is now in writing

Today's real news is about whoⓘ pays. A confidential prospectus from Anthropic, reported by Reuters, sets out at least $518 billion of AI infrastructure spending over a decade across six partners, with about 80% of it non-cancelable or payable whether the capacity is used or not. The same company reported a net loss of $42 billion for 2025.

The mix inside that book matters for merchant chips. The largest single piece is about $161.2 billion of Broadcom equipment lease obligations, against up to $84.5 billion for Nvidia-based capacity and more than $20 billion of capacity from Advanced Micro Devices. Custom silicon is no longer a side bet at the margin; it is the biggest line.

The wider picture rhymes. The five largest American cloud providers have added roughly $750 billion to their combined 2026 and 2027 investment plans, taking the two-year total to about $1.89 trillion, a 66% rise since the start of this year. By the same account the group would need about $308 billion of annual AI revenue to break even, against an estimated $183 billion earned today, and two-thirds of that revenue comes from just two firms. Supply is being built against promises, not receipts.

4

What to watch next

Three things could change the answer.ⓘ First, the financing. Nvidia is in early talks with insurers and a reinsurance broker about covering lenders when a small cloud provider defaults and the chips pledged against the loan cannot be resold for enough to repay it. No deal has been agreed. Watch whether such cover is actually written, because that is the test of whether banks still believe the collateral.

Second, China. Beijing has signalled it will let ByteDance, Alibaba and other domestic firms buy a new Nvidia workstation-class part that executives expect to fall outside American export limits, and ByteDance is reported to be planning an order of one million of them. Nvidia's China revenue has already dropped to $4.55 billion from $9.66 billion a year earlier, and Nvidia and Advanced Micro Devices are lobbying to narrow China provisions in next year's defence bill. Licence wording, not appetite, decides whether any of that volume ships.

Third, the sites. Bain projects $5 trillion to $6.5 trillion of data-centre spending by 2030 and says the industry would need $6 trillion of annual revenue by 2031 to justify it, with today's consumer and business AI services covering up to $1.8 trillion. It also counts $68 billion of American projects blocked or delayed in the June quarter on local opposition and resource limits. Even a kinder memory market would not help if there is nowhere to plug the racks in.
